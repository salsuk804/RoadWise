The signs you will meet on Pakistani roads

Pakistan's signs follow the British system, set out in the national Manual of Signs, Signals and Markings. Most carry text in both English and Urdu. Artwork varies a little between cities, and many signs are faded, damaged or missing, so ride as if a sign could be absent.

Who goes first: priority signs

Two signs decide who has right of way. Both are common at junctions in every city.

Never do this: prohibitions

A red ring means it is forbidden. Breaking one is a punishable offence.

You must do this: mandatory signs

A solid blue disc is an order. White symbol on blue means follow it.

Slow down, something is ahead: warnings

A red-bordered triangle warns of a hazard. Slow down before you reach it.

Good to know: information signs

Blue squares and rectangles guide you. No action is required, but they tell you what is nearby.

Rules in a nutshell

Open any question for the short answer.

Who has right of way at a junction without signals?

The vehicle already on the main road, and traffic from your right when both arrive together at a roundabout. When unsure, slow down and let the other go. Being right is no use if you are hurt.

How far behind the vehicle ahead should I stay?

At least three seconds. Watch the car ahead pass a fixed marker, then count. If you pass the marker before three, drop back. Make it six seconds in rain.

On which side do I overtake?

Pakistan drives on the left, so you overtake on the right. Only do so on a broken line with a clear view, and return to your lane when you can see the vehicle's headlights in your mirror.

When must I use my lights?

From dusk to dawn, in fog, heavy rain or dust. Low beam only when other traffic is close, and dip for oncoming vehicles.

Do I need to stop for an ambulance?

Yes. Pull to the left, slow or stop, and let it pass. Never follow it closely to clear traffic for yourself.
