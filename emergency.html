When things go wrong, and how to be ready

In an emergency you do what you practised.

Three things that can happen at speed

Open one at a time. Each shows what you feel, what to do first, and the instinct to resist.

A tyre bursts

What you feel: the handlebar shakes or the vehicle pulls hard to one side.

Keep a firm, relaxed grip. On two wheels, squeeze the tank with your knees. Let speed bleed off gradually, then brake gently once you are slow and steer to the left shoulder.

The brakes stop working

What you feel: the lever or pedal goes soft and the vehicle does not slow.

Pump the brake rapidly, since this can build pressure back. Downshift one gear at a time to use the engine for braking. Steer toward gravel, an uphill slope or open ground if you can. Use your horn and lights to warn others while you slow down.

Watch: what to do when your brakes fail. Open on YouTube

Oncoming high beams blind you

What you feel: flash blindness for several seconds.

Do not look into the lights. Look to the left edge of the road and follow the edge line. Slow down slightly while your eyes recover. Do not flash back at the other driver, because two blinded drivers is worse than one.

You arrive at a crash. What first?

Rule one: do not become the second casualty. The order below is a real sequence.

  1. Make the scene safe. Park ahead with hazards on. Place a warning triangle or signal well behind the crash.
  2. Call for help. Dial 1122, 115 or 130 on a motorway. Give the exact location.
  3. Control bleeding. Press firmly on the wound with clean cloth. Do not remove it if blood soaks through, add more on top.
  4. Leave the helmet alone. Do not remove it unless the person cannot breathe and you cannot clear the airway. Moving the neck can worsen a spinal injury.
  5. Stay and talk. Keep them still, calm and warm until trained help arrives.
